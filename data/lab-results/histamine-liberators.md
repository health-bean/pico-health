# Histamine liberators: what the evidence says

A "histamine liberator" is a food that is said to make the body's own mast cells release histamine, whatever the food's own histamine content. Many low-histamine diets tell people to avoid these foods.

## The short answer
The idea is widely repeated but has **almost no direct evidence**. No human clinical study shows that a particular food releases histamine in the body. Food lists vary from one source to the next, and nobody has worked out how the effect would happen. If Pico shows these foods, it should present them as "clinical opinion, test your own tolerance", not as fact.

## What the sources say (all read this session)
- **German/Austrian/Swiss guideline (Reese et al., Allergo J Int 2017; Allergol Select 2021):** This is the official clinical guideline. It names no foods and directly criticises the idea, saying there is "no reliable evidence of the existence of 'histamine liberators' in foods nor of their clinical significance".
- **Vlieg-Boerstra et al., Neth J Med 2005 (abstract):** The only systematic search of the evidence. It found no double-blind food-challenge studies, only a few in-vitro and animal studies. It calls the role of these diets "hypothetical but worthy of further investigation".
- **Sánchez-Pérez et al., Nutrients 2021:** Names citrus fruits, seafood, papaya, tomato, nuts, pineapple, spinach, chocolate and strawberries, "among others". It adds that there are no human clinical studies supporting the idea.
- **Comas-Basté et al., Biomolecules 2020:** Names papaya, kiwi, strawberry, pineapple and plum, and says the mechanism "has not yet been elucidated". (This is the same Barcelona group as Sánchez-Pérez, so the two papers are not independent.)
- **Zhao et al., Biomolecules 2022:** Its Table 2 lists foods that "promote the release of histamine": citrus fruits, papaya, strawberries, egg whites, chocolate, nuts, fish, pork, cheese, fermented sausage, green peppers, wheat germ and bean sprouts. The table mixes classic liberators with putrescine-rich foods (green peppers, wheat germ, bean sprouts, cheese, sausage). It gives no evidence-strength statement.
- **Zuberbier et al., JACI 2002 (abstract):** In patients with chronic urticaria, tomato did trigger reactions. However, tomato extract did **not** release histamine from mast cells directly; the authors blamed volatile aroma compounds. This is evidence against the "liberator" explanation for tomato.
- **Bartko et al., Alcohol 2016 (abstract):** Not about food liberators. It found that ethanol does not inhibit the histamine-breaking enzyme DAO in vitro.
- **Hrubisko et al., Nutrients 2021:** Mentions the concept but gives no list of liberating foods.
- **Maintz & Novak, AJCN 2007:** This is the source of the famous liberator table, but it **could not be read**: the publisher's site blocked automated access with a CAPTCHA. Nothing was recorded from it. Someone should check it by hand.

## Catalog foods named in at least one paper
- **Named in 3 papers:** Papaya, Strawberries
- **Named in 2 papers:** Pineapple, Dark Chocolate / Cocoa
- **Named in 1 paper:** Kiwi, Plum, Tomato, Spinach, Pork, Bell Pepper ("green peppers"), Wheat Germ, Bean Sprouts

Some items are food groups whose members the papers never name, so they are not linked to catalog foods: citrus fruits, nuts, seafood, fish, cheese, fermented sausage. Egg white is not matched to whole Eggs.

## What isn't known
- Whether any of these foods releases histamine in people at normal portion sizes.
- How it would happen, if it does.
- Why the lists disagree. Most lists repeat each other or trace back to older diet handouts rather than to primary studies.
