# Oxalate — Siener et al. (Univ. Bonn) papers

## Used
1. **Siener R, Seidler A, Hönow R. Oxalate-rich foods. Food Sci Technol (Campinas). 2021;41(Suppl 1):169-173.** DOI 10.1590/fst.10620 (SciELO; CC BY). Full text read. Table 1 (Fabaceae) and Table 2 (Convolvulaceae, Malvaceae): soluble + total means, n. Rows: White Beans, Kidney Beans, Green Beans, Red Lentils, Chickpeas, Sweet Potato, Okra, Dark Chocolate / Cocoa.
2. **Siener R, Hönow R, Seidler A, Voss S, Hesse A. Oxalate contents of species of the Polygonaceae, Amaranthaceae and Chenopodiaceae families. Food Chem. 2006;98:220-224.** DOI 10.1016/j.foodchem.2005.05.059. Paywalled, not read. Values taken as quoted in #5, Table 3 (ref [143]): Spinach, Sorrel, Rhubarb, Swiss Chard (mangold), Beet, Amaranth, Quinoa, Buckwheat (total only).
3. **Siener R, Hönow R, Voss S, Seidler A, Hesse A. Oxalate content of cereals and cereal products. J Agric Food Chem. 2006;54:3008-3011.** DOI 10.1021/jf052776v, PMID 16608223. Paywalled. Wheat wholegrain flour 70.0 and Bulgur 59.4 as quoted in #5 Table 3 (ref [144]); whole grain T. aestivum 53.3 from the abstract. (The Norton errata already holds this paper's wholemeal toast 40 mg row, which is not repeated here.)
4. **Siener R, Seidler A, Voss S, Hesse A. Oxalate content of beverages. J Food Compos Anal. 2017;63:184-188.** DOI 10.1016/j.jfca.2017.08.005. Paywalled. Pils beer 1.3 and white wine 0.3 mg/100 mL as quoted in #5 Table 2 (ref [60]); the white-wine value is also in the abstract.
5. **Siener R. Nutrition and Kidney Stone Disease. Nutrients. 2021;13(6):1917.** DOI 10.3390/nu13061917, PMC8229448, CC BY 4.0. Review used only as a source quoting the originals above (Tables 2 and 3; refs [60], [143], [144], [145] checked in its reference list).

## Looked at, not used
- Siener R, Seidler A, Voss S, Hesse A. The oxalate content of fruit and vegetable juices, nectars and drinks. J Food Compos Anal. 2016;45:108-112 (10.1016/j.jfca.2015.10.004). Paywalled; the juices it reports aren't catalog foods. Its "soybean drink, 62% soymilk" (4.4 mg/100 mL) is not plain soy milk, so it was skipped.
- Hönow R, Gu KL, Hesse A, Siener R. Oxalate content of green tea… Urol Res. 2010;38:377-381 (10.1007/s00240-009-0245-x). Paywalled; the abstract and review give only ranges.
- Siener R, López-Mesas M, Valiente M, Blanco F. Oxalate content in herbal remedies and dietary supplements. J Med Food. 2016 (10.1089/jmf.2015.0068). These are supplements, not catalog foods, and the abstract gives ranges only.
- Review (#5) values citing Hönow & Hesse 2002 (ref [55]): sesame, almond, hazelnut, pistachio, black pepper, parsley, star fruit, kiwi, fig, blackberry, olive, coffee, cocoa range. Left to the other helper, as instructed.
- #5 ranges only (red wine, black/green tea, cocoa 567-619): skipped.
- #1 non-catalog or unclear matches: quail beans, brown lentils (catalog has Green/Red only), licorice root, blue fenugreek (T. caerulea, not fenugreek T. foenum-graecum), dry soybeans/flakes/flour (no catalog soybean), fava beans, whole dry green peas (not clearly "Split Peas").
- Cereal paper's wheat bran (457), couscous (65.2), T. durum (76.6) and T. sativum (71.2): no matching catalog food, or the species name was ambiguous.
- No Siener paper on herbs/spices, nuts, soy products or plant-based milks turned up in Europe PMC or Crossref. The hinted "Nutrients 2021 Oxalate content of herbs and spices" does not appear to exist.
